## 3. Findings, ranked by cost

### F1 — Wait-spinning is still live and is the expensive failure (7.88% of fleet tokens)

Measured on **0.4.739 / 0.4.741 / 0.4.743 / 0.4.744** — i.e. releases *without* `wait-spin-guard`,
which first ships in the 0.4.744→0.4.748 range.

- `waiting_tokens` **852,482,742** = **7.88%** of the 10.82B fleet tokens in `waits`
  (noop alone 6.83%). Against the published baselines: 18.3% (24h to 09-07), 1.44% (24h to 09-08).
- **1,498 noop turns**, 312 poll turns. Top commands: `echo .` ×666, `echo idle` ×360,
  `date -u +%H:%M` ×181, `gh pr checks 584` ×99.
- Adoption: **23 of 55 waiting runs used `wp-await`**; 32 runs still spun.
- Worst single run: `monorepo1` `agent-ab977725ea58298d8` (`general-purpose`) — **668 noop turns,
  465,922,553 tokens, `await_calls=0`**, `echo .` ×666.

**Direction:** this is what the 0.4.748 upgrade actually addresses. Rolling `monorepo*` and
`monorepo-nx*` forward is the highest-value action in this report.

### F2 — Stranded stops are REAL but RARE — ~1.3/day, and ~0 tokens

The shape that prompted the audit: an agent ends its turn asserting a wait with **nothing pending**
to re-invoke it. `waits` cannot see this (a stranded agent emits nothing), so it was measured with a
purpose-built detector: last assistant turn bare + terminal wait language + no `Agent`/`Task` spawn +
no `run_in_background` Bash + no later background-task/SYSTEM NOTIFICATION wake-up.

- **4 candidates in 72h**, out of 180 subagent runs. **0 main sessions** (of 37).
- **All four are `agent_type: general-purpose`** — the `/full-cycle` workers. Zero reviewers.
- Spread across `monorepo-nx4` (0.4.743) ×2, `monorepo2` and `monorepo3` (0.4.744). **Not
  version-correlated**, and present on releases both with and without `wait-spin-guard`.
- Only one is unambiguous on its closing sentence: `monorepo3` `agent-a68e09972ccc286c`, 93 turns,
  ending *"Still pending — will report once CI completes."* The other three matched within the last
  1500 chars, not the final sentence.

**Cost profile is the inverse of F1**: a strand burns **~0 tokens** (the agent is stopped) and an
unbounded amount of **wall clock and human attention** — it is noticed only when Dean looks.

**MACHINE_ASLEEP is separable and does not explain these.** `cycletime` reconciliation **PASS**
(worst drift 0.0s), `pmset` coverage 1.0: 59.09h / **4.0%** of 1,460h wall clock. So "the laptop was
off" is a real 4% of the fleet's clock but is not what stranded these four runs.

### F3 — The proposed Stop-hook guard does not survive its own measurement

The detector above **is** the guard's proposed detection logic, run offline. Its precision is the
finding:

| pass | candidates | genuine | precision |
|---|---|---|---|
| broad wait-language regex | 6 | 1 | **17%** |
| tightened + terminal-state regex | 4 | 1 clear, 3 unclear | **≤50%** |

A `Stop`/`SubagentStop` hook that blocks a stop costs a **full turn (~557k tokens)** on every false
positive. At ~1.3 genuine strands/day and 50%-at-best precision, it would spend more tokens on
wrongly-refused stops than the stranding costs — and stranding costs ~0 tokens to begin with. **The
economics invert the case for building it.**

What survives: restricting to `agent_type == general-purpose` removed **every** reviewer false
positive in both passes. If anything is built, that population — 1/45th of runs, and 100% of the
observed strands — is where it belongs, and the `/full-cycle` SKILL contract is the cheaper lever
than a new hook event.
